Safety

One person reviews reports. Not a team, not a shift — one person, in one timezone, working through a queue in the order things arrive. Usually that means a few days. That is the honest expectation and not a service level, and you should read everything below knowing it.

Nothing here is decided by software. There are no keyword filters, no classifiers, and no automatic account actions — everything is reported by a person and judged by a person. Two narrow exceptions exist and are listed at the bottom of this page.

Report something on Docket

You need an account, and you can report what you can see.

Profiles, posts, replies, job postings and company pages all carry a report link. So do messages in a thread you are part of — and only those: a thread you are not in cannot be reported, because it cannot be read. That is not an oversight to be fixed later. It is the design.

What counts as breaking a rule is a short, closed list. Read the member conduct policy — everything it does not name is not an offence.

Intimate images

If an intimate image of you is on Docket, there is a separate form, and you do not need an account to use it. It is deliberately not the ordinary report route.

The content is hidden when the report arrives, before anyone has looked at it — if you can tell us where it is precisely enough for us to find it. A person then either confirms the removal or puts it back. Both you and the person who posted it are told.

How Docket handles your data

A complaint about data protection is a different thing again, with its own form and its own legal clock. No account needed for that one either.

If you have no account

For illegal content you have seen without signing in, write to address. The same address is the contact point for authorities.

This instance has not published a contact address, which means there is nobody named to answer a report sent from outside. That is a gap in how it has been set up, and saying so is better than printing an address that reaches no one.

What happens after a report

You get an acknowledgement straight away, and a second message with the decision and the reasons for it. If something of yours is removed or restricted, you are told what was done, why, and how to appeal — once. An appeal is the same person looking again with new information. Docket is not going to pretend it has an independent panel.

What Docket will not do: quietly reduce who sees your posts, or shadowban. There is no ranking to turn down, and hiding a decision from the person it was made about is a lie about their own account. Removals are total and you are told about them.

Counts of what has been reported and what was done about it are published on the transparency log, with no names.

The two things that are automatic

Every image uploaded is checked against hashes of known child sexual abuse material, and against a local list of images taken down here under the intimate image route, before it is stored. That is the complete list of automation. Neither is a judgement about what anyone said.
