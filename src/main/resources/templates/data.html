Your data

Everything Docket knows about you, and the way out. Neither one costs anything, and neither depends on the other.

Take a copy

One file with everything in it: your profile, your posts and replies, who you are connected to, the jobs you applied for and what happened to them, the recommendations people wrote about you, your pictures, and your conversations — whole, both sides.

It comes as a zip. Inside there are pages you can read and a JSON file for a machine, plus a note about what we use your data for, who else sees it, how long we keep it, and what is not in there and why.

Other people's words are in there. Your conversations come out whole because half a conversation is not a record of anything. They are yours to keep — not to publish, and not to build a picture of someone who did not ask you to.

Leave Docket

You can delete your account whenever you want. You do not have to download anything first.

What goes. Your profile — your name, headline, where you are, your summary, your positions, your education, your skills and your photo. The posts you wrote. Your saved posts and saved searches. Your connections and any requests. Your job applications. You stop appearing in search, and your profile page stops existing.

What stays. Your side of every conversation stays where it is, marked as written by a former member. So do the recommendations you wrote about other people, and the replies you left under other people's posts. This is the honest part: you cannot completely disappear from Docket. The alternative would be letting one person delete a colleague's correspondence into a monologue of holes, and that is somebody else's record, not yours to erase.

Backups. We take backups, like anyone who does not want to lose your data to a broken disk. When you delete your account, your data goes from Docket straight away. In the backups it is put beyond use — nobody reads it, nobody restores it, and it is not used for anything — until those backups roll over on their normal schedule, and then it is gone from those too.

If you think we got this wrong. You can complain to us about how we have handled your data. We will acknowledge it within 30 days and tell you what we decided. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office, whether or not you complain to us first.
